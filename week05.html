3D Scanning & SLA Resin 3D Printing

Comprehensive development log detailing optical 3D facial digitization of a team member, point cloud mesh processing, slicing parameters for Stereolithography (SLA/DLP) photopolymer resin printing, post-processing wash/cure cycles, and dimensional accuracy validation.

Institution: Universidad de Cuenca • Academic Program: Industrial Engineering (6th Cycle) • Prototyping Facility: FabLab Cuenca

1. Facial 3D Scanning & Mesh Processing Workflow

3D scanning provides a non-invasive workflow to digitize organic geometries with high spatial accuracy. To test resolution limits and surface continuous contours, a team member's face was scanned using optical 3D capture techniques. This serves as an anatomical baseline for ergonomics and custom pediatric fitting protocols.

1

Face Capture & Lighting Protocol

Executed non-contact optical 3D scanning under indirect, diffuse LED illumination to minimize specular reflections on skin. Kept the subject completely stationary while capturing 360-degree overlapping point cloud depth maps.

2

Mesh Processing & Alignment

Imported raw point data into MeshLab and Blender. Filtered out ambient noise, aligned multiple sub-scans using Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithms, and repaired non-manifold edges to achieve a closed manifold surface.

3

Export & Scale Verification

Extracted a watertight mesh, verified real-world anatomical bounding box dimensions using physical caliper measurement points (interpupillary and nasal distance), and exported high-density STL/OBJ files for additive slicing.

Scanning Bottlenecks & Practical Solutions

  • Facial Micro-Movements & Involuntary Blinking: Human subjects exhibit natural involuntary twitches during prolonged scans, causing ghosting artifacts. Using an accelerated multi-pass sweep strategy and fixing the subject's head against a rigid support eliminated tracking alignment loss.
  • Hair & Eyebrow Specularity: Dark hair and shiny skin surfaces disperse optical light patterns, creating open holes in raw point clouds. Applying Screened Poisson Surface Reconstruction filters closed boundary loops while retaining high-frequency facial feature details.

2. Additive SLA/DLP Resin Manufacturing & Post-Processing

Stereolithography (SLA) Photopolymerization Process

Stereolithography (SLA) utilizes UV light to selectively cross-link liquid photopolymer resin layer by layer. Unlike standard FDM printing, resin technology offers isotropic mechanical strength, smooth surface finish without visible layer lines, and sub-100 micron tolerance execution—making it ideal for fine anatomical replicas and micro-geometries.

Layer Height 0.05 mm 50 micron Z-axis layer resolution
Photopolymer Material 405 nm Standard Rigid / Tough Clear Resin
Exposure Timing 2.5 s Normal layer (30s on bottom layers)

SLA Post-Processing, Safety & Chemical Wash Protocol

  • Chemical Washing (IPA 99%): Uncured liquid monomer resin remaining on surface features was dissolved using a 2-stage Isopropyl Alcohol bath (6-minute automated agitation cycle).
  • UV Post-Curing Chamber: Placed the cleaned model inside a 405nm UV curing chamber for 15 minutes. This ensures complete polymerization, maximizes tensile strength, and neutralizes liquid resin toxicity.
  • Support Touchpoint Removal: Micro-supports located on the non-critical back geometry were carefully clipped using flush cutters and wet-sanded with 1000-grit sandpaper for an unblemished aesthetic surface.
