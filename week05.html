Assignment 05

IKEA Case: Flat-Pack Bear Chair, Parametric Design, Laser Cutting & Packaging

Design of a bear-shaped children's chair for an 8-year-old girl following the IKEA flat-pack concept: from hand sketch to CAD, laser cutting, packaging box and assembly manual.

Institution: Universidad de Cuenca • Academic Program: Industrial Engineering (6th Cycle) • Prototyping Facility: FabLab Cuenca

1. Design Brief: The IKEA Flat-Pack Case

The assignment asks us to apply the IKEA concept: products designed to be disassembled and packed in flat boxes, which drastically reduces storage and transport costs. Our product is a children's chair in the shape of a bear, designed for an 8-year-old girl, together with its packaging box and its assembly manual.

Requirement 1

Appealing for children

A bear-shaped chair designed for an 8-year-old girl.

Requirement 2

Joint-based assembly

The chair assembles and disassembles using joints only, with no glue.

Requirement 3

Flat-pack in a box

All parts lay flat and fit inside a purpose-designed box.

Requirement 4

Assembly manual

A step-by-step instruction manual, accessible through a QR code, comes with the package.

Requirement 5

Parametric design

The chair and the box are both modeled with parametric dimensions.

2. Design Process: From Hand Sketch to CAD

Stage 1: Sketching and Parametric CAD Modeling

Hand sketch first, then refinement as the shapes were seen on screen

We started with a hand sketch of the bear chair to define its general shape and character. We then rebuilt it in CAD and improved the design according to how it looked on screen, adjusting proportions and details until the shape worked as a flat-pack product. Finally, the parts were laid out flat, ready for cutting.

6. Lessons Learned and Conclusions

What we learned

A hand sketch does not translate one-to-one into CAD: improving the design on screen was a necessary step. We also learned to think about packaging from the start, since the shape of the parts determines how well they fit flat inside the box.

Mistakes and difficulties

The first sketch had to be adjusted once we saw it in CAD. Making a bear shape that is attractive and, at the same time, assembles without glue was the main challenge.

Conclusions and recommendations

Designing for flat-pack forces a balance between appearance, strength and packaging. We recommend validating the joints on a small test cut before cutting the full chair, and designing the box and the manual in parallel with the product.
